If you dictate for a living using Dragon Professional Anywhere or Dragon Legal Anywhere, there is a date on the calendar you need to know about.

Nuance has advised partners that both products are being retired. Dragon Professional Anywhere and Dragon Legal Anywhere reach **end of sale on 31 December 2026** and **end of life on 31 December 2027**. Dragon Anywhere Mobile, the companion app for iPhone and Android, is on a shorter timeline again: end of sale 30 June 2026, end of life 30 June 2027.

That is not a small change. For a great many Australian professionals — solicitors, barristers, consultants, report writers, government staff — the Anywhere products have been the day-to-day dictation tool for years. Understanding what the two dates actually mean, and planning around them properly rather than at the last minute, is the difference between an orderly transition and a scramble.

## What Has Been Announced

Two dates matter, and they mean different things.

**End of sale (31 December 2026).** This is the last day new licences can be purchased and existing contracts can be started or renewed. After that date, no new subscriptions and no renewals. Existing agreements can be extended one final time during 2026, and those extensions then run out during 2027.

**End of life (31 December 2027).** This is the point at which the product stops being a going concern. Subscription licences run to this date at the latest. Purchased on-premise licences receive maintenance up to this date, after which all maintenance and security updates cease.

The practical consequence of the end-of-life date is the one people underestimate. After 31 December 2027 there are no security updates, no bug fixes and no support. Continuing to run a purchased, self-hosted licence past that point may be technically possible, but for any organisation with compliance obligations — a law firm, a government department, a medical practice, anyone handling client or patient information — running unsupported software that touches sensitive dictation is not a defensible position.

It is also worth being clear about what is *not* affected. Dragon Medical One continues as Nuance's cloud product for the medical market and is unaffected by this announcement. The desktop products, Dragon Professional 16 and Dragon Legal 16, also remain available — though it should be said plainly that a locally installed desktop product is not a like-for-like replacement for the cloud and mobile workflows that many Anywhere users specifically moved to.

No successor product to the Anywhere line has been announced.

## What Your Options Are

Realistically there are three paths, and which one suits you depends on how you actually work.

### Move backwards to Dragon Professional 16 or Dragon Legal 16

This is the option Nuance partners will most often present, and for some users it is genuinely correct. If you work at a single Windows PC, you want deep voice command and control of the machine, you use custom macros and scripted commands, and you were only on Anywhere for licensing reasons rather than mobility, then the desktop product will do what you need.

Be honest with yourself about the trade-offs, though. Dragon Professional 16 is a locally installed product with a voice profile. That means a profile that lives on one machine, that can become corrupted, that needs to be backed up and migrated when you change computers, and that requires the install-limit and activation management that drove a lot of people to the cloud products in the first place. Our [Dragon Professional 16 comparison](/comparison) sets out the specifics.

There is also the obvious strategic question. Dragon Professional 16 was released in 2023. Having just watched an entire product line be retired with no successor, committing to another locally installed Dragon product is a judgement call about how long that line will itself remain supported.

### Move to a modern cloud dictation product

The closer structural match to what Anywhere users actually had — cloud processing, no local voice profile, works across machines — is a current-generation cloud dictation product. This is where [speech to text software](https://www.speechrecognition.cloud) built on modern speech models has changed the landscape considerably since the Anywhere products were designed.

Speech Recognition Cloud is the option we see most Anywhere users land on. It is an Australian product from Voice Recognition Australia, it runs as a small Windows application that types directly at the cursor in whatever program you are using, and it requires no voice training and no profile at all. Because there is no profile, there is nothing to corrupt, nothing to back up and nothing to migrate — you sign in on any machine and start dictating.

The automatic punctuation is worth calling out specifically for anyone coming from an older Dragon workflow. You dictate naturally and the punctuation is inserted for you, rather than speaking "comma" and "full stop" through every sentence. For long-form work — file notes, letters of advice, reports, correspondence — that is a meaningful change to how the work actually feels.

### Do nothing yet

Defensible until roughly mid-2027, and not beyond. The thing to avoid is arriving at December 2027 having not tested anything. A dictation change is not just a software swap; there is a period of getting used to a different rhythm, working out microphone placement, building any custom vocabulary you need. Give yourself months, not weeks.

Two honest caveats on that table, because a comparison that only flatters one side is not much use to anyone.

**Dragon Legal Anywhere's legal vocabulary is a real thing.** If you dictate citations, case names and Latin terminology all day, the legal edition's vocabulary handling is a genuine advantage. Speech Recognition Cloud supports custom vocabulary, so you can add the terms you use most, but that is you building a list rather than the product arriving with a legal corpus already trained in. For a barrister dictating judgments and submissions, test it against your actual work before deciding.

**Deep voice command and control is not matched.** The Anywhere products let you drive the Windows interface by voice — opening applications, navigating menus, running scripted step-by-step commands. Speech Recognition Cloud is a dictation tool. It types where your cursor is, extremely well, in any Windows application. It does not run your computer. If you rely on hands-free control for accessibility reasons, that distinction is the most important line in this article, and Dragon Professional 16 on the desktop is likely your path rather than any cloud product.

For everyone else — and that is most people, most of the time — the question is whether $159 a year of [voice to text software](https://www.speechrecognition.cloud) that will still exist in 2028 does the job that $795 a year of software with a fixed expiry date is currently doing. For a large number of Anywhere users, it does.

## What To Do Next

If you are on Dragon Professional Anywhere or Dragon Legal Anywhere, three practical steps.

**First, find out what your renewal date is and when your current term ends.** If your subscription renews in 2026, that renewal is your last one. Know whether it takes you to mid-2027 or the end of 2027, because that is your actual runway.

**Second, test an alternative on your real work, not on a demo sentence.** Dictate an actual letter, an actual file note, an actual report. Accuracy on the vocabulary you genuinely use is the only test that matters. Speech Recognition Cloud has a free edition with 20 minutes a month and no credit card required, which is enough to form a real opinion.

**Third, sort out your microphone before you judge any software.** More apparent accuracy problems come from a poor microphone in a noisy room than from the recognition engine. If you are running a laptop's built-in microphone, you are not testing the software, you are testing the microphone.
